Background & User Guide

A tool for calculating the probability of a true biological change between two consecutive laboratory results, based on the theory of Reference Change Values.

🔬 Background

When a patient's laboratory result changes between two time points, the question arises: does this difference reflect a genuine biological change, or is it simply the result of natural variation and measurement imprecision?

Classical laboratory practice uses a fixed threshold — the Reference Change Value (RCV) — to answer this question with a binary yes/no. The RCV is calculated from two sources of variation:

RCV = 2.77 × √(CVi² + CVa²)
CVi — intra-individual biological variation (the natural fluctuation within a person over time)
CVa — analytical variation (the imprecision of the measurement method)
2.77 — factor corresponding to a two-sided 95% probability threshold (Z = 1.96)

If the percentage difference between two results exceeds the RCV, the change is considered significant at the 95% level. However, this binary approach discards valuable probabilistic information.

📊 What this app does differently

LabTracker replaces the binary RCV threshold with a continuous probability. Instead of asking "did the result cross the threshold?", it asks: "what is the probability that this change is real?"

The calculation uses the same underlying formula, but converts the Z-score to a probability via the standard normal distribution:

Z = |Δ%| / (√2 × √(CVi² + CVa²))
Δ% — percentage difference between the two measurements
The probability of a true change = 2 × Φ(Z) − 1
where Φ is the cumulative normal distribution function

A result of 84% means there is an 84% probability that the observed difference reflects a true biological change rather than noise. This is statistically analogous to 1 − p-value, expressed as a percentage for clinical intuition.

Note: this framing is a deliberate simplification for clinical usability. A strict frequentist interpretation of p-values differs from a direct probability statement. The goal is a tool that is intuitive and practically useful, not a formal hypothesis test.

⏱ Time-dependent biological variation

For several analytes, biological variation (CVi) is not constant — it increases with the time interval between measurements. As more time passes, more biological change accumulates. LabTracker incorporates time-dependent CVi models for 19 analytes, including ALT, cholesterol, HbA1c, HDL, LDL, triglycerides, platelets, CK, creatinine, iron, and others.

For these analytes, the app shows a Days between measurements input field. Entering the number of days between the two results adjusts the CVi accordingly, using validated mathematical models (logarithmic, quadratic, or step functions) derived from published biological variation data.

📱 How to use the app

  1. 1Search for an analyte in the search bar. Results are sorted with exact matches at the top.
  2. 2Select the analyte. The reference values for Maastricht University Medical Center (MUMC+) are shown immediately.
  3. 3Enter Measurement 1 (the earlier result) and Measurement 2 (the later result). Both must be positive numbers in the same unit as the reference values.
  4. 4For analytes with time-dependent CVi, enter the number of days between the two measurements.
  5. 5The probability of a true change is calculated and displayed instantly as a percentage with a colour-coded bar.
  6. 6Optionally, click Show details to see the absolute difference, percentage difference, Z-score, and the classical RCV 95% threshold.
  7. 7Click Show biological variation (CVi) to inspect the CVi value used in the calculation.

✦ Key features

🏥 MUMC+ reference values
Reference ranges from Maastricht University Medical Center displayed for every analyte.
⏱ Time-dependent CVi
19 analytes have dynamic biological variation models that adjust based on the interval between measurements.
🎯 Probability output
Continuous probability instead of a binary threshold — more informative for clinical decision support.
📋 MUMC+ analyte list
Only analytes measured at MUMC+ CDL-Chemistry are included, keeping the app focused and relevant.
🔬 CVa correction
Analytical variation is capped at 0.5 × CVi (desirability criterion), except for Na, Cl, and Ca where CVa = CVi.
📄 Linked thesis
The PhD thesis by Hilderink et al. underpinning the biological variation models is accessible directly from the app.
